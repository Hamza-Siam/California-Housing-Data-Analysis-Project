# California Housing Data Analysis Project

* <h4><b> Overview </b></h4> 

  The project analyzed a housing dataset to explore various factors influencing house prices and living conditions. Key insights include the high concentration of houses near the ocean, with most residents living within an hour of the coastline. A notable relationship between ocean proximity and house values was identified, with houses near islands being relatively more expensive. The analysis also highlighted the impact of housing age, with older homes correlating to higher prices in coastal areas. Additionally, the study uncovered that households with fewer than 1,000 total bedrooms dominate the housing market, and median income plays a significant role in house prices.

* <h4><b> Key Findings </b></h4> 

  - <b> High concentration of houses near the ocean: </b> Most homes are located within an hour's drive of the coastline.
  - <b> Proximity to the ocean influences house prices: </b> Houses near the ocean, especially islands, tend to be more expensive.
  - <b> Older homes correlate with higher prices in coastal areas: </b> Age of the house influences price, with older homes in coastal regions being priced higher.
  - <b> Smaller households dominate the market: </b> Homes with fewer than 1,000 total bedrooms represent the majority of the housing market.
  - <b> Median income impacts house prices: </b> Higher median income is associated with more expensive homes.

* <h4><b> Insightful Details </b></h4> 

  - <b> Proximity to the Ocean Drives Prices – </b> Homes closer to the coastline, especially on islands, tend to have significantly higher prices.
  - <b> Median Income is a Strong Predictor – </b> Higher median income in an area correlates with more expensive housing, making it a key factor in price estimation.
  - <b> Older Coastal Homes Retain Value – </b> Unlike inland areas, older homes in coastal regions often have higher prices, possibly due to location desirability.
  - <b> Smaller Households are More Common - </b> The majority of homes have fewer than 1,000 total bedrooms, suggesting a market trend favoring smaller living spaces.
  - <b> Population Density Affects Pricing – </b> Areas with high population density often show lower house prices, indicating affordability concerns in crowded locations.

* <h4><b> Challenges </b></h4> 
 
  - <b> Missing or Incomplete Data – </b> Some attributes have missing values, requiring imputation or removal to avoid bias.
  - <b> Outliers in Pricing & Income – </b> Extreme values in house prices and median income may skew analysis, requiring careful handling.
  - <b> Geospatial Influence on Prices – </b> House prices are highly dependent on location, making spatial analysis complex but essential.
  - <b> Feature Selection Complexity – </b> Identifying the most relevant variables for price prediction is challenging due to multicollinearity.
  - <b> Bias in Dataset – </b> The dataset is based on California’s 1990 census, which may not accurately reflect current market conditions.
